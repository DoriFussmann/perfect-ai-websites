## What These Tools Actually Do (and What They Don't)

If you've decided it's time to start measuring your business's presence in AI-generated answers, you're already ahead of most small business owners. The harder question is: which tool do you buy, and do you even need to buy one right now? The answer engine optimization tool market has expanded rapidly, and walking into it without a framework almost guarantees either overspending on enterprise features you'll never use or picking a tracker that tells you what's happening but can't help you change it.

AEO tools split into two camps: watchers and movers.
 That's the clearest organizing frame for any small business evaluating this category. 
Some tools only monitor citations, while others help you change them through optimization workflows.
 Before comparing specific platforms, it helps to understand the four jobs these tools perform — because no single tool does all four equally well, and most small businesses only need one or two of them at the start.

The first job is **prompt and AI rank tracking**: are you being cited at all when someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI Overviews a question in your category? The second is **citation and source analysis**: when the AI does cite someone, which third-party pages — Reddit threads, industry directories, review sites — is it pulling from, and are any of them yours? The third is **content optimization**: how should your pages be restructured so AI engines can extract a direct answer from them? The fourth is **end-to-end platforms** that attempt all three in one workspace. Most small businesses should start with tracking before investing in anything more complex.

## Free and Freemium Starting Points

Before spending anything, two free tools provide a legitimate baseline that most business owners overlook. 
Bing Webmaster Tools is among the easiest free ways to see whether AI is citing your business, and its June 2026 update added Intents, Topics, Citation Share, and Compare — built on an AI Performance report that covers Copilot, Bing, and partner AI. On reporting depth it is well ahead of Google's newer Search Console AI report, which is thinner and initially limited to a UK preview.

For the first time, the Bing tool lets you understand how often your content is cited in generative answers, with clear visibility into which URLs are referenced and how citation activity changes over time.

The Google side of the equation is evolving. 
Bing Webmaster Tools launched its AI performance report in February and is available globally, while neither report currently includes click data.
 That's a meaningful limitation: 
impressions without clicks tell you where you're being seen, not what that visibility is worth.
 Still, for a business that has never measured AI citations at all, these two free tools together establish a baseline that no paid platform can replicate because the data comes directly from the search engines themselves. HubSpot entered the AEO space in October 2025 by acquiring XFunnel — one of the first platforms purpose-built for AI answer engine monitoring — and integrating it directly into Marketing Hub.

HubSpot's free AEO Grader runs a one-time check of how ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini describe your brand, with no account required, returning a score out of 100 across five weighted dimensions including sentiment, brand recognition, and share of voice.
 This makes it a practical first audit for any business that has never thought about AI visibility before. Importantly, the grader gives a useful "outside-in" look at your brand from the perspective of what AI systems already believe about you — before any deliberate optimization effort.

## Dedicated AEO Platforms Worth Knowing

Once you've established a free baseline and confirmed that AI visibility genuinely matters for your market, dedicated paid tools unlock more granular tracking. The most-reviewed platforms in this category each have different strengths.

### Otterly

Otterly AI tracks brand mentions and citations across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity, with pricing that runs from $29 per month on the Lite plan up through Standard and Pro tiers, and it holds a 4.9 out of 5 rating on G2.

It earns its "otterly simple" tagline: setup takes minutes, the interface is clean, and it monitors four base engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Copilot — while pairing monitoring with actionable recommendations, content briefs, and crawlability checks.
 For a solo business owner or small team just entering AEO measurement, 
Otterly at $29 per month is a defensible place to test whether this channel matters before committing to more.

There is one important caveat. 
Otterly turns your SEO keywords into prompt formats rather than showing what real users are actually asking and how often, so prioritization stays a judgment call.
 That distinction — between synthetic prompts and real user queries — turns out to matter more than most tool comparisons acknowledge.

### Peec AI

Peec AI offers tracking across up to 10 AI models, including ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, and Grok, with multi-language and multi-country tracking built in.

Peec AI does not offer content auditing or optimization — it measures.
 That narrowness is actually a strength for businesses that already have a content execution plan and simply need reliable, broad-coverage tracking data to monitor progress. 
If the need is dependable visibility reporting, and the content and technical work lives elsewhere, Peec AI does that job well.

### Geoptie

Geoptie is an AI search visibility platform that tracks how answer engines mention and cite a brand and then turns what it finds into content work needed to close the gaps. It covers seven platforms — ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, and Microsoft Copilot — with any four selectable per brand on every paid plan, including the $49 entry tier, which carries 15 tracked prompts and no per-engine surcharge.

Geoptie provides a free GEO audit tool alongside its paid content studio, and that free audit evaluates your content's readiness for generative AI consumption, including schema depth and semantic structure.
 For very budget-conscious teams, this combination of a free audit and a $49 paid entry makes it one of the more accessible all-in-one options in this category.

### AirOps

AirOps grew up as a content automation platform and added AI visibility afterward, and content throughput is still its strong suit. AirOps Insights tracks citation rate, mention rate, and sentiment across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.
 The platform is best suited to teams whose primary bottleneck is producing enough AI-citable content at scale, rather than teams that are still figuring out where they stand. 
AirOps covers only four answer engines and, notably, bases prompt "popularity" on an estimate rather than real conversations
 — a limitation to weigh carefully before committing.

### Profound

Profound is an enterprise-grade AEO platform that tracks how brands appear across 11 AI surfaces — including ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Copilot, Grok, Meta AI, DeepSeek, and Amazon Rufus.

It is best-in-class for two things: the depth of its prompt-intelligence dataset, sourced from real user conversations rather than synthetic queries, and its enterprise security posture.
 However, pricing tells a clear story about who the platform is actually built for. 
Profound costs $99 per month for Starter — which tracks ChatGPT only — $399 per month for Growth covering three AI search engines, and custom Enterprise pricing for the full 10-plus engine set, which third-party reviews report at $2,000 to $5,000 or more per month.

For most small businesses, the $99 Starter tier tracks only ChatGPT, and reaching the multi-engine coverage that makes Profound valuable requires custom Enterprise pricing. Small teams also tend to be overwhelmed by the dashboards and lack the analyst time to use them.

## The Synthetic-Prompt Problem — and Why It Changes Your Selection Criteria

One of the most overlooked factors when comparing AEO tools is how their prompts are generated. Most tools in this space construct their test questions one of two ways: by using synthetic queries generated from your keyword data, or by drawing from real user search behavior. The difference is not cosmetic.

There is a hard line between platforms watching what people actually ask answer engines and those inferring demand from an SEO keyword database. The first tells you what to work on; the second gives you a modeled guess.
 Research into synthetic versus real query datasets confirms this matters in practice: 
real queries are significantly more likely to begin with direct fact-seeking terms, and synthetic models may not fully replicate this behavior.
 A platform that tracks AI-generated questions nobody actually asks can make your visibility score look healthier than it is, directing your content efforts toward topics your customers are not pursuing.

There is also the related problem of AI answer variability. 
Run the same prompt twice and you may get different citations. Any tool that does not account for this by running multiple passes and averaging the results is presenting noise as signal — so it is worth asking directly: how many times does the tool run each prompt, and what does it report?
 For small businesses making content investment decisions on a limited budget, acting on noisy data is an expensive mistake.

## Citation and Source Analysis: The Underused Job

Beyond tracking whether you appear, citation analysis reveals *which pages* the AI is relying on when it answers questions in your category — and those pages are often not yours. 
One platform found that a single third-party site with only 16 pages mentioning a brand generated over 1,400 AI citations. Your own website may be doing less work than you think.

This is where source analysis tools earn their place. Understanding that an AI is primarily citing a competitor's review profile, an industry directory, or a specific forum thread tells you where to focus your authority-building efforts — whether that means earning more third-party mentions, improving your presence on specific platforms, or filling content gaps on pages that are already being indexed. 
If you want buyers to find and trust your brand in ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, Gemini, and Copilot, you need clear facts, source-backed claims, and consistent brand details across your site and trusted third-party pages.

## How to Choose Without Overspending

Matching a tool to where you actually are in your AEO journey is the most practical way to avoid wasted spend. 
Most small teams can start meaningfully for under $100 per month in tooling, with content and outreach effort layered on top. The biggest cost is usually the time required for consistent content production, not the software.

A sensible progression looks like this: start with the free layer — Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report and HubSpot's AEO Grader — to confirm your baseline and determine whether AI citations are already happening, whether they're accurate, and whether competitors are showing up where you aren't. If that data surfaces a clear gap, a low-cost paid tracker at the $29 to $49 range is a reasonable next step. 
Budget tools like Otterly at $29 per month or Geoptie's starter plan around $49 per month cover the gap between free manual checks and a full mid-tier platform. Starting free, then upgrading only once you've confirmed AEO visibility actually matters for your specific market, avoids paying for enterprise-grade citation tracking before you know what you're tracking toward.

Before committing to any paid platform, watch for pricing structures that look affordable until you read the fine print. 
Add-ons and base-subscription requirements often push the real price two to three times above the advertised number. Several platforms treat their base subscription as a gateway rather than a complete product, then charge extra once you try to use the features that actually matter to your team.

## Tools Measure — They Don't Fix

There is a gap in the AEO tool market that most comparison guides skip over: 
the AEO tools that generate real value connect gap discovery to the content workflow — you find the visibility gap, brief against it, publish the content, and track whether the gap closed. Tools that stop at the report create dashboards that sit unused.

This points to a structural limitation that small businesses need to budget around honestly. A tracking platform tells you that your pages are being skipped in AI answers for a given category of question. It can flag that your schema markup is incomplete, that AI crawlers may not have reliable access to your site, or that your content lacks the answer-first structure AI engines prefer to extract from. What it cannot do is fix any of those things. 
No CMS ships native AEO features yet, so the strongest setup pairs your CMS with a dedicated AEO platform.
 And a dedicated AEO platform paired with a website that lacks proper structure, schema, crawl access, and topically organized content is a monitoring tool pointing at problems it cannot solve.

This is the honest argument for treating website infrastructure and content architecture as the execution layer that sits beneath any tool decision. The most sophisticated AEO dashboard in the market cannot rebuild a poorly structured website, add the missing schema markup across dozens of pages, create the pillar-and-cluster content architecture that AI engines favor when deciding which sources to trust, or ensure that your site is technically accessible to the crawlers AI systems use to stay current. 
HubSpot's 2026 State of Marketing data found that 49% of marketers had seen search traffic decline because of AI-generated answers, and 58% described AI referral traffic as high intent
 — meaning the traffic that does come from AI citations converts at unusually high rates. A tracking tool tells you how much of that traffic you're missing. It takes an execution layer to actually capture it.

If a monitoring platform surfaces real gaps — poor citation rates, AI crawlers being blocked, content that fails the answer-first structure test — the practical next question is not which tool to upgrade to, but whether the website itself is built in a way that makes improvement possible. For a business with an outdated or poorly structured site, tool investment without infrastructure investment is measurement without the ability to act.

## Key Takeaways

- AEO tools divide into four jobs — tracking citations, analyzing citation sources, optimizing content structure, and end-to-end platforms — and most small businesses should start with citation tracking before adding complexity.
- Free options from Bing Webmaster Tools and HubSpot's AEO Grader provide a genuine no-cost baseline; paid platforms in the $29–$49 range make sense only after that baseline confirms a real gap exists.
- The most-reviewed dedicated platforms — Otterly, Peec AI, Geoptie, AirOps, and Profound — each occupy different positions on the tracking-versus-optimization spectrum, and Profound's small-business pricing ($99 Starter covers ChatGPT only) places it firmly in the enterprise segment for meaningful coverage.
- Whether a tool uses real user prompts or synthetic AI-generated queries is a critical selection criterion: synthetic-prompt tools can inflate apparent visibility scores by tracking questions nobody actually asks.
- AEO tools identify problems but cannot fix them — a monitoring platform without website infrastructure capable of supporting the changes it recommends is an expensive gap analysis, not a solution.
